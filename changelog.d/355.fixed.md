- **A sign can recover within the call at the first failover after cosigners restarted.** The
  driver keeps one channel per cosigner and sends nothing over the channels of the cosigners that do not lead. A
  cosigner that restarted meanwhile, above all one that came back under another address, left its
  channel unusable for one call: when the leader then went down, the driver failed over to
  cosigners that were up, every attempt failed with `UNAVAILABLE: io exception` before the request
  was sent, and the sign ended with "no cosigner left to try" (0.5.0-rc.1 during a rolling restart;
  Canton's retry succeeded). Now a call that failed at every cosigner can make one more attempt, at
  a cosigner its request was never sent to: it waits for one of their channels to reconnect, at
  most one attempt deadline and within `request-timeout`, and asks that cosigner once. If no
  channel reconnects in that time the call fails as it would have, and Canton retries it. This
  second pass does not repeat a request at a cosigner that may have seen it. It applies to signs
  and to the other calls that share the sign's failover walk; the generation of a hosted key, the
  group-key agreement and calls on an EC hosted key (ECIES) are unchanged. A call that fails at
  every cosigner while the request to at least one of them could not be sent (a cosigner that is
  down, a TLS handshake that fails) now takes up to one attempt deadline longer to fail.
- **The driver logs why an attempt failed at the transport level.** The WARN line of a failed
  attempt names the exception under the gRPC status (`UNAVAILABLE: io exception; cause:
  AnnotatedConnectException: … Connection refused: <host>/<address>`) and says when the request
  was not sent.
